## What I learned

**Routing is the real pain, and Elaine proved it herself.** She tried to set up her own TVC last year. It took two months to find the right form. The eMPF platform sorts trustee forms by English letter, not by scheme, so you cannot find yours. She then contacted customer service, waited two more weeks, and was sent a link to the eMPF website. The forms have to be done on the eMPF site and cannot be done on a phone.

She tested MPFA's own AI chatbot. It returns links, but only to a generic menu page, not into the platform. Her words: it is useless. Karen's framing for this project is "how do we avoid being that useless."

Her instruction for the bot: reach a specific endpoint. Name the form. Do not leave the member on a home page.

**Most calls are about things that are no longer theirs.** Withdrawals, fund changes, contributions all go to eMPF. Members still call BOCIPT out of habit, because they have been with them for years. First contact usually lands on PS, who answer what they can and then redirect.

**The call centre is the emotional risk.** They cited a bank that spent tens of millions on an AI bot, still needed humans at the end, and found that customers arrived at the human already angry from being bounced around the bot. The complaints were not about the AI. They were about the state the customer was in by the time a person picked up.

Their position: do not let the bot die, but do not force it either. Keep the hotline early on. If the numbers show the bot works, then withdraw the hotline.

**Their own tools are broken and nobody noticed.** A projection tool on the website cannot complete its last step. She reported it to Ken. There were no complaints, because members who could not press the button just gave up.

**Their staff will not use fancy software.** People who have been with Bank of China for 20 to 30 years work in Excel. Give them a clever app and they say they do not understand it. Give them something resembling what they already use and they adopt it.

**They want a picture, not a data dump.** On fund analysis: "I am not an analyst. I just want to know why. Give me a chart." Their current comparison output is a static PDF in a regulator-mandated format that is identical for every trustee and cannot be embellished or reduced.

**They do not know their own new products are out.** My Choice added three funds this year. Nobody knows. The comparison vendor does not even include them. Her point: if they had a bot, they could tell members. Right now they publish and nobody sees it.

**Content is too long.** Bank of China people repeatedly say their materials have too much in them. Their internal view is that Hong Kong-written content is not as polished and needs specialist editing. She wants optimisation that does not disturb compliance.

**Prospect data is an opportunity they have not used.** They want to capture consent before someone opens an account, so they can store and analyse who is interested. Customer data cannot currently go straight to the bank. This ties into decision 9 and 10.

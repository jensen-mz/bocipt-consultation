# How to gather, then what to fill

The three frameworks are one empty form. Take the empty form into the room. Each box is a question. Write their words in the box. Do not collect notes and sort them later.

You fill it in this order. Do not skip ahead.

1. Wednesday, with Elaine: why, and the real member problems.
2. 12 Oct: the member's day, and which ideas sit on that day.
3. 21 Oct: the line of what will actually be delivered, and one sentence of "done" for each item above the line.

CSI writes the technical spec from what is above the line. You check their spec against those sentences.

## The whole picture

```
Why her team is judged
  → Who feels that
    → What they would do differently
      → The problems members actually have
        → The member's day, left to right
          → A line: above is this funding delivery, below is later
            → One done sentence per item above the line
```

## 1. Impact map

This answers why anything should be built.

Four boxes, in this order:

| Box | Meaning | What you ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why | One result she is judged on | "What number does your manager ask about every month?" If that is hard: "If this works in six months, what would your officers stop doing?" |
| Who | The person who feels that number | "Who feels that: the member, your officer, or you?" |
| Change | What that person does differently on a normal day | "What would they do on a Tuesday morning that they do not do now?" |
| Deliver | A bot behaviour that causes that change | Do not fill this on Wednesday. This is where a funding idea is allowed to sit later, and only if it causes the change. |

How to get it out of her: one question, then wait. When she answers, repeat her words and write those words. If she jumps to a feature ("we need a chatbot that…"), ask "What happens today when that goes wrong?" Stay on the job, not the tool.

Leave Wednesday with one Why and two or three Changes. If you have five Whys, you do not have one yet. Ask which single one she would sign.

## 2. Opportunity tree

This is the list of problems, before any solution.

The Why from the impact map sits at the top. Under it, write problems in her words. A problem is something a member or an officer struggles with. A problem is not a feature from the slides.

What you ask, after the Why is written:

- "Tell me about the last time a member called and your officer already knew the answer."
- "What do they ask before they have logged in?"
- "What do they ask after they have logged in?"
- "What do you wish they could finish without your team?"

Write each answer as a short problem: "Member does not know what MPF is." "Member cannot find their balance." "Officer repeats the same form explanation."

If she names a slide idea, ask "Which of these problems would that remove?" If she cannot point at one, park the idea. Do not argue. Parked is not deleted.

How many: five to ten problems is enough. Stop when the stories start repeating.

## 3. Story map

This is the picture of what will be delivered.

On 12 Oct, take the problems from Wednesday and lay them left to right in the order a member meets them:

- Before login
- After login
- A prompt the bot would start, which is Level 3

Under each step, place only the problems that happen there. Then, and only then, place a funding idea under the problem it serves.

Draw one line across the map.

- Above the line: what Pension will stand behind for the regulator, and what CSI will be asked to deliver.
- Below the line: later. Still visible. Not deleted.

What you ask the room:

- "Walk me through a member who knows nothing. Then one who is logged in. Then one who should have been nudged."
- "If you sign one short list at the end of October, which of these cards are on it?"
- For each card above the line: "How would you know this one worked?"

The last answer becomes one sentence: who, in what situation, and what result Elaine would accept.

Example: "A member who has not logged in asks what MPF is. The bot answers from our FAQ and does not tell them which fund to buy."

21 Oct only locks the line and those sentences. It does not reopen the whole map.

## Rules while you are in the room

- Write their words, not a cleaner version.
- One question, then silence.
- Ask for a recent real case, not a view about AI.
- Do not teach what AI can do until a problem is on the wall.
- Do not ask her to pick from the 10 slides.

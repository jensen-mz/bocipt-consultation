## 1. Domain cheat sheet — what BOCIPT actually is

Read this once before you go in. Ten minutes.

**Structure**
- JV set up in 1999 between subsidiaries of Bank of China and Prudential plc.
- Two companies: the Trustee (BOCIPT, our client) and the Asset Manager.
- The Trustee's job: safeguard scheme assets, trust and custody services, fund administration, and member/employer servicing.
- The Asset Manager's job: investment management, selling and marketing the funds, SFC-licensed.
- Practical effect: a member asking "how do I withdraw" is a trustee question. A member asking "which fund should I pick" is an investment-manager question. That boundary is exactly where a chatbot can get into trouble. Worth remembering.

**The schemes BOCIPT is trustee of**
- My Choice MPF
- BOC-Prudential Easy-Choice MPF
- Plus occupational schemes: BOCHK Staff Provident Fund, Government Employees MTS, Prudential HK Provident Fund / Pension Plan, VTC Provident Fund.
- Easy-Choice is the one run with an agency, with commission. My Choice is run in-house. That was said in the 2 Oct briefing.

**The big one: eMPF Platform onboarding, 5 June 2025**

Both MPF schemes moved onto the government's eMPF Platform on 5 June 2025. This changed Elaine's team's job more than anything else in the last two years.

- Since onboarding, routine scheme administration — contributions, withdrawals, transfers, account and balance enquiries, forms — goes through the **eMPF Platform**, and members are told to call **eMPF's hotline 183 2622**, not BOCIPT's.
- BOCIPT keeps trustee-specific and fund-specific matters: its own enquiries, complaints, the schemes it still services, and the occupational schemes.
- BOCIPT's own hotlines: Easy-Choice **2929 3030**, My Choice **2929 3366**. Counter and mail: Suite 1507, 15/F, 1111 King's Road, Taikoo Shing.
- eMPF has its own service centres (Wan Chai, TST East, Tsuen Wan) and its own pledge: acknowledge written enquiries in 3 working days, reply in 12, answer the hotline within 2 minutes. **Those are the eMPF Platform Company's standards, not BOCIPT's. Do not quote them to Elaine as her KPIs.**

Industry context, NOT BOCIPT-specific: a Legislative Council reply (22 Oct 2025) reported roughly 2,400 complaints **platform-wide** from eMPF's launch in June 2024 to end-September 2025, over 90% answered within the pledge, average 10–12 working days. The platform operator raised its enquiry and complaint headcount by more than 60% over three months. BOCIPT onboarded on 5 June 2025, near the **end** of that window, so these complaints cannot be attributed to BOCIPT's migration. Use as background only.

Why this matters to you: a share of the calls hitting Elaine's team may be **misrouted** — members who should be calling eMPF on 183 2622, or who called eMPF and were bounced back. That is a question, not a claim: "How many calls are people who should have called 183 2622?" If the answer is large, "tell the member which channel owns this task" is a strong first use case — cheap, low-risk, and easy to defend to MPFA.

This is directly relevant. A chatbot on their official site is, in part, an anti-scam measure: an authoritative voice members can reach without leaving the real site. It also means the bar for "does this look official and safe" is high with this client, and any bot that gives a wrong answer on an official domain is a fraud-adjacent risk. ## 6. The chain — eight links

This is what you draw from her answers. Every use case that reaches CSI must have all eight links filled. A missing link is not a detail, it is the reason a project drifts.

| # | Link | The question that produces it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Her number** | "What are you measured on?" | Complaints resolved within 7 business days |
| 2 | **Incident** | "Tell me about the last time…" | Member rang, had already tried the website, still could not find what to do |
| 3 | **Problem** | Group the repeated incidents | Members cannot tell which channel handles their question |
| 4 | **Member job** | "What were they ultimately trying to get done?" | Wants to withdraw on retirement, without getting it wrong |
| 5 | **Owner** | "Who is actually allowed to answer this — you, eMPF, or the Asset Manager?" | eMPF Platform owns the transaction; BOCIPT can only route |
| 6 | **Bot capability** | One of: answer / route / retrieve / refuse / escalate / log | Route |
| 7 | **Done sentence** | Who + situation + result, and what it must not do | A member asks about withdrawal; the bot states which channel owns it and links there. It does not process the request and does not advise |
| 8 | **Proof** | "How would you know this worked?" | Misrouted calls fall; her team's handle time falls; the 7-day pledge gets easier to hit |

**Link 5 is the one that matters most here.** This client has a three-way split that did not exist before June 2025:

- **BOCIPT (Trustee)** — trustee and fund-specific enquiries, complaints, the occupational schemes.
- **eMPF Platform** — routine administration since 5 June 2025: contributions, withdrawals, transfers, balance, forms. Members are directed to 183 2622.
- **BOCI-Prudential Asset Management** — which fund to pick, investment performance, selling.

A bot that answers across that boundary is a compliance problem, not a feature. So every candidate gets an owner stamped on it before it goes on the line. This also produces the cheapest high-value use case they have: **the bot's first job may simply be to tell the member who owns their question.** That is link 5 turned into a product.

Above the line: goes to MPFA at the end of October, and to CSI to build.
Below the line: later. Still visible. Not deleted.

**Bucket** — MPFA's own three words, from the sandbox announcement:
- Customer experience — the member gets an answer without a call.
- Operational efficiency — her team spends less time repeating itself.
- Protection / anti-fraud — the member is not misled, and gets a safe authoritative answer.

Aim to finish with at least one card in each bucket. That is a credible October story in the regulator's own language.

**Line** — in October / later / parked. Parked means no owner, or no incident behind it, or nobody can name a proof.

### Three worked chains

**A. Misrouted call** (efficiency + experience)
Her number: 7-business-day complaint resolution · Incident: member rang three times about a withdrawal · Problem: members cannot tell which channel owns what · Job: withdraw on retirement without getting it wrong · Owner: eMPF owns the transaction, BOCIPT can only route · Capability: route · Done: a member asks about withdrawal, the bot names the channel that owns it and links there; it does not process and does not advise · Proof: fewer repeat calls, shorter handle time.

**B. Fake-site check** (protection / anti-fraud)
Her number: none yet — this is the one to co-create with her · Incident: ask for one; they publish fake-site warnings, so one probably exists · Problem: members cannot tell the real BOCIPT from a fraudulent site · Job: confirm this is genuine before acting · Owner: BOCIPT · Capability: answer, from approved text only · Done: on the official domain, a member asks whether a site or message is real; the bot states that bocpt.com is the only official site and gives the hotlines; it does not assess any specific link · Proof: fewer fraud-related contacts. This card is your strongest MPFA story, because anti-fraud is a named focus area of the sandbox.

**C. "Show me my balance"** (the trap)
Incident: member calls asking for their balance · Problem: balance is not visible to them where they expect · Job: check my money is there · **Owner: eMPF Platform, since 5 June 2025. Not BOCIPT.** · Capability: route — not answer · Done: the bot tells the member balance enquiries go through eMPF and links there. It does not display account data · Proof: balance calls stop arriving on the BOCIPT hotline.

Card C is why link 5 exists. Read as a feature request it looks like a Level 2 login integration — expensive, and it touches member data, which cannot leave the data centre yet. Read with the owner stamped on it, it collapses into a routing answer you could ship in weeks. Same incident, completely different requirement.

### Where the chain breaks

| Symptom | What is missing | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card has a capability but no incident | Nobody actually has this problem. It came off a slide | Park it |
| Card has an incident but no proof | Cannot be shown to work, so cannot be shown to MPFA | Ask "how would you know?" again, or park |
| Card has no owner | Nobody can be held to it | Find the owner or park |
| Card's proof is "members will like it" | Not a number | Ask what she would count |
| Every card is customer experience | No efficiency or protection story | Go back to Part C of the questions |

Above the line on 21 Oct, one card each, with all eight links. CSI writes the technical spec and picks the stack from those cards. You check their spec against the **done sentence** and the **proof** — not against the slides, and not against your own reading of the meeting.
